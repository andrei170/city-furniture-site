What AI Tells Your Customers Today

More buyers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity "is City Furniture any good?" before they visit a store. We asked all three on 2026-06-14. They agree on your strengths, and they agree on one wound.

ChatGPT

"A solid mid-market store, locals recommend it. But it ranks RK Furniture Gallery above City Furniture for quality, and tells buyers to compare the two before buying a sofa."

Gemini

"Good price-to-quality and friendly staff, but 'Skip the appliances: buy major appliances from a dedicated appliance retailer.' Get delivery terms in writing and inspect on arrival."

Claude

"A sofa or mattress is probably fine. But for appliances with warranty concerns, read the small print carefully before committing. After-sale service is where people run into issues."

The wound: three engines steer buyers away from appliances, the category in your name.

All three pull the same theme from BBB and Birdeye: appliance warranty hand-off and delivery disputes. One literally tells shoppers to buy appliances somewhere else, and another ranks a competitor above you. This is not a star rating you can ignore. It is an AI assistant talking your highest-ticket customer out of the sale, every day, and right now you cannot see it happening.

Why It Is a Problem, and How It Compounds

Left alone, this does not stay still. It is a loop that feeds itself, and every turn makes the next one worse.

1A handful of unhappy appliance and delivery reviews land on BBB, Yelp and Birdeye.
2Google's AI overviews and the chat engines scrape and cite those reviews at query time.
3AI starts telling buyers to skip your appliances and ranks competitors above you by name.
4Fewer appliance buyers walk in. Fewer happy customers means fewer fresh positive reviews.
5The negative becomes the dominant signal, the AI verdict hardens, and the loop restarts louder.
Meanwhile the competitor who does manage this pulls further ahead every quarter, because the engines learn to recommend them instead of you.

Priority Roadmap (your order, from the call)
Phase 1: Foundation
  • One central brand presence. A real City Furniture Canada corporate identity: one main Instagram, one Facebook, one brand home everything posts under.
  • Fix the website. Per-store inventory (not Kamloops stock for everyone), presentable, and findable. Diagnose the Google findability bug first.
  • Stand up measurement. GA4, Meta Pixel and CAPI, call tracking, and a per-store dashboard so every owner sees their own numbers.
  • Pick a 2-3 store pilot to prove the system before rolling to all 13.
Phase 2 & 3: Engine On, Then Scale
  • Paid media engine. Meta first (FB/IG, local radius per store), Google next. Funded in part by vendor co-op, claimed and reported.
  • Per-store local pages with LocalBusiness schema, claimed Google profiles, and a post-purchase review engine in every city.
  • Content bank + UGC. One shared library every store feeds, the "tag your room" referral program, and a yearly promo calendar.
  • Founder series. The 50-year "how we built this" story as premium organic fuel the paid program amplifies.
  • AI search & reputation control. Separate your brand from the Florida chain, answer the reviews the AI engines read, build fresh positive volume, and monitor what the engines say, on your dashboard.
